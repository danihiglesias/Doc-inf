# Thesis types

There are two options for the doctoral thesis: the traditional monograph thesis (writing an original manuscript covering the whole research process of the thesis work) and the compendium of publications. The compendium is currently the most effective option since, with at least 3 published JCR articles, you only need to write a brief summary to present the thesis; the traditional manuscript is not needed.

```{admonition} Recommendation
:class: tip

My initial recommendation will always be to go for a thesis by compendium of publications, as it carries more prestige and means that the journals' external reviewers are already assessing and validating your work. The monograph option is always available and you do not need to inform the Doctoral School (Escuela de Doctorado) until the time of thesis submission. You can start the thesis with a compendium in mind and, if the time runs out without the 3 publications, switch to a monograph.
```

## Monograph or traditional thesis

The monograph thesis presents the research in a single integrated document, usually organised into chapters on introduction, background and theoretical framework, objectives, methodology, results, discussion and conclusions. The doctoral student must show the coherence of the work and justify its original contribution to knowledge. This format allows the research to be developed with its own structure, without each chapter having to correspond to a published article. Choosing this format does not remove the programme's publication and activity requirements, which must be met before applying for thesis submission: at least one published JCR article.

## Thesis by compendium of publications

The thesis by compendium of publications brings together publications arising from the same research and integrates them through a dissertation that explains their objectives, methodology, the relationship between the works and the overall conclusions. It is not simply a matter of attaching articles: it must show that they form a coherent and original piece of research. This format requires authorisation and meeting additional conditions on the number, quality and authorship of the publications. The general USAL procedure requires **at least three contributions** published or accepted after starting the doctorate, with the student being first author on at least two, with exceptions subject to express assessment. It also requires authorisations from the co-authors and a waiver from co-authors without a doctorate to use those works in another thesis. Programmes may add specific requirements, so it is advisable to agree on this format with your supervisors from the initial planning stage.
